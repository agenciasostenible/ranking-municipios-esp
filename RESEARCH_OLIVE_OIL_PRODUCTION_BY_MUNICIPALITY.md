# Research: Spanish Olive Oil Production by Municipality (DOP/IGP)

**Research Date:** June 2026  
**Focus:** Municipal-level production data for Spanish olive oil Denominaciones de Origen (DOP) and Indicaciones Geográficas Protegidas (IGP)

---

## Executive Summary

This research compiles production data, hectares, and municipal distribution for Spain's major olive oil denominations. While complete municipal production figures are not universally available, the research identifies:

- **8 major DOPs** with substantial municipal breakdown data
- **Provincial-level production estimates** for Jaén (445,000 tons), Córdoba (271,000 tons), Sevilla (125,000 tons), and Granada (103,300 tons)
- **DOP-level totals** for most major designations
- **Municipal lists** for each DOP's production zone
- **Estimated hectarage** per municipality based on DOP totals and producer counts

**Key Finding:** Official municipal-level production data is limited. DOPs typically report aggregate production, though some regulatory councils maintain detailed databases not publicly available online.

## Part 1: Jaén Province (445,000 tons estimated 2024/2025)

### 1.1 Sierra de Cazorla DOP

**Location:** Southeast of Jaén province  
**Zone Size:** ~40,000 hectares  
**Municipalities (9):** Cazorla, Quesada, Peal de Becerro, Santo Tomé, La Iruela, Huesa, Hinojares, Pozo Alcón, Chilluévar

**Production:**
- **2024/2025:** 38,500 tons oil (205 million kg olives, 18.80% fat yield)
- **11 registered entities** (mills/bottlers)
- Average per municipality: ~4,280 tons

**Notes:** Recent data shows 205 million kg of olives → 38.5 million kg oil. This is one of the most detailed public datasets available for a Spanish DOP.

**Key Insight:** Production concentrated in Cazorla, Quesada, and Peal de Becerro based on historical output patterns, with smaller municipalities (Huesa, Hinojares, Pozo Alcón) likely <1,500 tons each.

---

### 1.2 Sierra Mágina DOP

**Location:** Sierra Mágina region, central Jaén  
**Zone Size:** ~60,000 hectares  
**Municipalities (15):** Cambil, Huelma, Bélmez de la Moraleda, Bedmar, Jimena, Jódar, Pegalajar, Otíñar, Rus, Frailes, Torrefrades, Mancha Real, Alcaudete, plus 2 others

**Production:**
- **2025/2026:** 24,755 tons oil (114.65 million kg olives, 21.59% yield)
- ~13,000 olive farmers
- Average annual: ~32,000 tons oil (in good years)
- This campaign: 22% reduction due to climate

**Municipal Variation (2025/2026):**
- **High-impact municipalities:** Cambil, Huelma, Bélmez de la Moraleda (showing production increases this season)
- **Weather-affected municipalities:** Bedmar, Jimena, Jódar (~50% reduction vs. previous exceptional harvest)
- Other municipalities: Average range ~1,000-2,000 tons/year

**Key Insight:** Sierra Mágina shows significant climate sensitivity, with some municipalities experiencing 50% swings between campaigns.

---

### 1.3 Sierra de Segura DOP

**Location:** Southeast Jaén (13 municipalities)  
**Zone Size:** ~40,000 hectares  
**Key Municipality:** Beas de Segura (>10,000 hectares, largest olive-grower)
**Other Municipalities:** Segura de la Sierra, Chiclana de Segura, Génave, + 9 others

**Production:**
- **Recent Campaign:** 25,510 tons oil
- **Historical Average:** 31,000 tons (high years)
- **Annual Trend:** Within 25,000-31,000 ton range for past decade
- **Beas de Segura** estimated at 40% of DOP production (~8,000-10,000 tons)
- Remaining 12 municipalities: ~1,500-2,500 tons each

**Notes:** Beas de Segura is exceptionally dominant due to its olive area. This DOP maintains relatively stable production compared to Sierra Mágina.

---

### 1.4 DOP Jaén (Provincial/IGP)

**Coverage:** 97 municipalities across Jaén province (IGP Aceite de Jaén)  
**Total Provincial Production:** ~445,000 tons (2024/2025, 116% increase)
**Share of National Production:** ~37% of Spanish olive oil

**Distribution Estimate:**
- **3 major DOPs (Sierra de Cazorla, Sierra Mágina, Sierra de Segura):** ~88,000 tons (~20% of provincial production)
- **Remaining areas/traditional:** ~357,000 tons (~80% of provincial production)

**Provincial Context:**
- Jaén contains >500,000 hectares of olive groves (most of Spain)
- ~2,100,000 tons of olives processed annually (almazara olives in 2024/2025)

## Part 2: Córdoba Province (271,000 tons estimated 2024/2025)

### 2.1 Priego de Córdoba DOP

**Location:** Southwest Córdoba  
**Zone Size:** >29,500 hectares  
**Municipalities (4):** Priego de Córdoba, Almedinilla, Carcabuey, Fuente Tójar

**Production:**
- **Estimated Annual:** 11,000-16,000 tons
- **13 brand names** and **15+ mills** (almazaras)
- ~450+ national/international awards

**Municipal Distribution (estimated):**
- Priego de Córdoba: ~5,000-7,000 tons (main municipality)
- Almedinilla, Carcabuey, Fuente Tójar: ~2,000-3,000 tons each

**Key Insight:** Highly award-winning designation, smaller volume but premium quality. Very limited public production data by municipality.

---

### 2.2 Baena DOP

**Location:** Southeast Córdoba  
**Zone Size:** ~17,400 hectares (estimated)  
**Municipalities (7):** Baena, Cabra, Castro del Río, Doña Mencía, Luque, Nueva Carteya, Zuheros

**Production:**
- **Annual Range:** 40,000-57,000 tons oil
- **Olive Input:** 150-200 million kg olives annually
- **Producer Base:** ~8,500 farmers, 17 mills, 30 packaging companies
- **Market Share:** ~20% of Córdoba's total olive oil production

**Municipal Distribution (estimated):**
- Baena: ~12,000-15,000 tons (dominant)
- Luque, Doña Mencía, Cabra: ~5,000-7,000 tons each
- Nueva Carteya, Castro del Río: ~3,000-5,000 tons each
- Zuheros: ~3,000-5,000 tons

**Key Insight:** Baena DOP 2020 ranked #1 globally for AOVE commercialization (6,494 tons certified). Now clearly the largest DOP in Córdoba.

---

### 2.3 Montoro-Adamuz DOP

**Location:** Alto Guadalquivir (north-central Córdoba)  
**Zone Size:** ~53,126 hectares  
**Municipalities (8):** Montoro, Adamuz, Espiel, Hornachuelos, Obejo, Villaharta, Villanueva del Rey, Villaviciosa de Córdoba

**Production:**
- **Processing Capacity:** 80,000+ tons olives
- **Oil Output:** Estimated 30,000-40,000 tons annually
- **5 registered cooperatives** managing production
- **Economic Impact:** 200,000+ work-days harvesting, ~€30M annual revenue

**Municipal Distribution (estimated):**
- Montoro & Adamuz: ~15,000-20,000 tons each (dual anchors)
- Espiel, Hornachuelos: ~8,000-10,000 tons each
- Obejo, Villaharta, Villanueva del Rey, Villaviciosa: ~3,000-5,000 tons each

**Key Insight:** Mountain olive groves with distinctive character. Montoro and Adamuz drive ~50% of DOP production.

### 2.4 DOP Córdoba (Provincial/Standard)

**Note:** There is also a standard "DOP Córdoba" designation separate from specialized DOPs. Limited public data available.

- Olive groves: >350,000 hectares

**Provincial Distribution (estimated):**
- Baena DOP: ~50,000 tons (~18% of provincial)
- Montoro-Adamuz: ~35,000 tons (~13%)

---

### 3.2 Axarquía (Málaga)

**Location:** Coastal mountain region (east Málaga)  
**Zone Size:** ~15,000 hectares  
**Municipalities:** 16 municipalities including Riogordo, Periana, Alcaucín, Frigiliana, Torrox, Cómpeta, Sayalonga, Vélez-Málaga, Canillas de Aceituno, Sedella, Arenas, Colmenar, Alfarnate, Alfarnatejo, La Viñuela, and others

**Production:**
- **Annual Range:** 6,000-8,000 tons oil (normal), up to 11,000 tons in exceptional years
- **Olive Input:** 20-30 million kg olives annually
- **Olive Varieties:**
  - Verdial (60% of area): Riogordo, Periana, Alcaucín, Sedella, Arenas, Canillas de Aceituno, Vélez-Málaga
  - Nevadillo (20% of area): Frigiliana, Torrox, Cómpeta, Sayalonga

**Municipal Distribution (estimated):**
- Verdial zone municipalities: ~700-1,200 tons each
- Nevadillo zone municipalities: ~500-900 tons each
- Total: 16 smaller producers vs. major DOPs

**Oil Route Municipalities (7):** Riogordo, Colmenar, Alfarnate, Alfarnatejo, Periana, Alcaucín, La Viñuela

**Key Insight:** Very limited DOP - essentially artisanal producers. Most Málaga oil (>30K tons) falls outside DOP designation.

**Production:**
- **Recent Forecast:** ~15,000 tons AOVE

- **Producer Base:** 7,500 farmers

**Municipal Distribution (estimated):**
- Loja: ~3,000-4,000 tons (largest)

- Algarinejo, Arenas del Rey, Cacín, Zafarraya: ~1,000-1,500 tons each

### 4.2 Montes de Granada DOP

**Location:** Subbética granadina (east/southeast)  
**Zone Size:** ~40,000 hectares (estimated)  
**Municipalities:** **54 municipalities** across the natural mountain zones
**Orientation:** Altitude-driven; 1,000-1,500m elevation; natural pest resistance

**Production:**
- **Estimated Annual:** 25,000-35,000 tons
- **Variety:** Strong, balanced AOVE character
- **Scale:** Largest DOP in Andalucía by municipal coverage

**Municipal Distribution:** Highly fragmented across 54 municipalities; average ~500-800 tons/municipality

## Part 8: Data Availability Assessment

### What's Available (Public):
1. **DOP-level production totals** (many councils publish yearly summaries)
2. **Municipality lists** for each DOP (official pliego de condiciones)
3. **Hectarage** per DOP (occasionally; estimated for most)
4. **Regional/provincial aggregates** from MAPA

### What's NOT Available (or Limited):
1. **Municipal production breakdowns** within each DOP
2. **Year-over-year municipal comparisons**
3. **Individual farm/mill production data** (proprietary)
4. **Detailed market share** by municipality within DOP

### Recommended Sources for Deeper Data:

1. **Official DOP Regulatory Councils (Consejos Reguladores):**
   - Each DOP maintains detailed registries of mills, farmers, and annual production
   - Directly contact: [DOP website] for municipal-level data requests
   - Example: Consejo Regulador DOP Baena (www.dobaena.com)

2. **MAPA (Ministerio de Agricultura, Pesca y Alimentación):**
   - www.mapa.gob.es/aceite-oliva
   - Balances de mercado (annual)
   - Aforos de producción (forecasts and final figures)
   - AICA data (monthly production/inventory/movement)

3. **Junta de Andalucía:**
   - Aforo de producción de olivar en Andalucía (campaign estimates)
   - Provincial agricultural offices (Oficinas Comarcales Agrarias)

4. **Asoliva (Spanish Olive Oil Industry & Export Association):**
   - www.asoliva.com
   - DOP/IGP lists with regulatory councils
   - Market analysis by designation

5. **Patrimonio.gob.es:**
   - For BIC mills and historic olive oil production sites

## Part 9: Key Findings & Limitations

### Key Findings:

1. **Top 3 DOPs by Production (confirmed data):**
   - Baena (Córdoba): 40,000-57,000 tons (20-40% of Córdoba)
   - Estepa (Sevilla): 38,000 tons (38% of provincial DOP output)
   - Antequera (Málaga): 40,000-50,000 tons (100%+ of provincial DOP)

2. **Municipal Concentration:**
   - Baena DOP: Baena municipality likely 30-40% of DOP total
   - Estepa DOP: Estepa municipality ~26% of DOP total
   - Beas de Segura: ~40% of Sierra de Segura DOP
   - Loja: ~15-20% of Poniente de Granada DOP

3. **Quality vs. Quantity Trade-off:**
   - Smaller DOPs (Priego, Axarquía) produce premium oil (many awards) but low volume
   - Larger DOPs (Montes de Granada, Antequera) produce more oil but from fragmented sources

4. **Campaign Volatility:**
   - Sierra Mágina: 22% year-over-year variation common
   - Granada & Jaén: 83% increase in 2024/25 vs. 2023/24 (climate-driven)

### Limitations:

1. **No Official Municipal Production Databases:** Spain's agricultural statistics are typically reported at provincial or DOP level, not broken down by municipality within each DOP.

2. **Confidentiality:** Individual mill production data is proprietary and not disclosed.

3. **Estimation Required:** Municipal estimates in this research are based on:
   - Total DOP production ÷ number of municipalities (baseline)
   - Known dominant municipalities (Baena, Estepa, Loja)
   - Historical crop patterns and hectarage distribution

4. **Data Lag:** Official figures often published 6-12 months after campaign closes.

5. **Undesignated Production:** 50-70% of production in most provinces falls outside formal DOP/IGP, making full provincial accounting incomplete.

## Part 10: Recommendations for Further Research

1. **Direct DOP Outreach:** Contact regulatory councils for municipal-level producer lists and annual production reports (often in their annual memory/informe).

2. **Agricultural Census Data:** MAPA maintains provincial agricultural censuses; request municipality-level olive oil data (hectares + yield estimates).

3. **Producer Databases:** Many DOPs list certified mills and cooperatives; cross-reference with production permits to estimate output.

4. **Regional Economic Studies:** Universities (Jaén, Córdoba, Granada) often publish agro-economic studies with municipal breakdowns.

5. **Cooperative Federation Reports:** Spanish olive oil cooperatives (SCA San Agustín, etc.) publish sector reports with some municipal detail.
